# Pair judgements: evidence and use

Schloss, K. B., & Palmer, S. E. (2011). *Aesthetic response to color combinations: preference, harmony, and similarity*, 73, 551–571. Published online November 2010. [Main paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13414-010-0027-0).

## Evidence digest

Forty-eight color-vision-screened participants judged 32 chromatic colors. Experiments 1–2 used 992 ordered figure–ground pairs: 100-pixel squares inside 300-pixel squares on gray, with measured display calibration. Experiment 3 used separated equal squares; Experiment 4 displayed eight figures simultaneously on colored backgrounds and used a different rating scale. Tasks occurred in separate, ordered sessions.

Pair liking and harmony generally increased with hue similarity. Liking additionally depended on component preferences and lightness contrast; harmony favoured lower saturation more. Harmony was not similarity: their lightness relationships differed. Average liking–harmony correlation was .79, while individuals ranged −.03 to .75. Foreground liking benefited from contrast, with hue-contrast benefits primarily at similar lightness. Complementary hues did not receive a general whole-pair advantage.

The final pair-preference regression explained 82.6% of average-rating variance using harmony, contextual foreground liking and ground liking. This is fitted explanatory variance, not prospective accuracy. Geometry, task instructions and larger palettes limit transfer; proposed causal mechanisms remained hypotheses. These experiments did not establish storytelling effectiveness or universal palette rules.

## Supplement check

[Supplement](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.3758%2Fs13414-010-0027-0/MediaObjects/13414_2010_27_MOESM1_ESM.pdf), especially S6–S9 and Table S1: the hue-contrast benefit is conditional, not absent whenever lightness differs. It was strongest for similar-lightness cuts, with weaker evidence for saturated/light pairings. Formal-training subgroup analyses used only 7, 9 and 6 participants at levels 1, 3 and 5. Personality correlations were not significant. Do not infer an individual's palette preference from personality or expertise.
