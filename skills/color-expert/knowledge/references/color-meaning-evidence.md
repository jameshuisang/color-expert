# Color meaning: evidence boundaries

Reviewed 2026-11-14; selected methods, results and discussion/limitations, not supplementary data or a reanalysis.

## Cross-cultural empirical study
[Jonauskaite et al., 2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/eprint/5G3HXWNGZTT6ZXFIHTDP/full), DOI 10.1177/0956797620948810.

4,598 participants across 30 nations and 22 native languages associated 20 emotion concepts with 12 color **terms**, not displayed swatches. Aggregate patterns were highly similar (mean correlation .88), with additional linguistic/geographic variation. Red was associated with both love and anger; Chinese participants additionally associated white with sadness. The authors explicitly distinguish conceptual association from actually feeling an emotion on seeing colored objects. Recruitment was voluntary and online, and country samples varied in size. These results support shared tendencies plus local variation, not a lookup table for every individual, exact hex value or national audience.

## Systematic review, not a new primary experiment
[Jonauskaite & Mohr, 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-024-02615-z), DOI 10.3758/s13423-024-02615-z.

The review included 132 English-language peer-reviewed articles from 1895–2022, covering 42,266 participants in 64 countries. Correspondences were generally many-to-many, involving lightness, saturation and hue rather than hue alone. Most evidence concerns abstract associations; transfer to experienced emotion and specific contexts remains unsettled. Preference and context-specific color effects were outside its focus. English-only publication selection, varied methods and inconsistent stimulus specification limit generalization. This is useful synthesis, not evidence that a blue UI causes calm, red raises conversion, or a particular palette is beautiful.

Keep the measurement type attached to every claim: term association, swatch association, felt-emotion report, preference or behaviour are different outcomes. Neither study establishes our design examples as experimentally effective.
